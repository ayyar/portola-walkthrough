# Ma Garden — a house for looking out

One focused Japanese-inspired design pass, developed from Quiet Lanterns / Set 1 with the latest R6 architecture and fixture orientation. The emphasis is on useful emptiness, quiet material differences and the changing garden. This is an independent proposal, not a claim to reproduce a particular designer's work.

## Arrival and the long hall

The earlier continuous white runner reads as a stripe pulling the eye down the entire corridor. Its broad lit lattice and contrasting counterwall feel more like a display passage than an arrival at home. Reduce the runner to a 2.65 m woven arrival mat and let the existing timber floor carry the rest of the journey.

Place a 1.18 m long, 0.40 m deep walnut shoe perch in the first substantial solid wall bay, centred approximately 4.70 m along the model's hallway. Its padded seat is about 0.49 m high, with enclosed shoe storage below and a small key tray at one end. Keep everyday items here, close to the entrance. Do not distribute consoles, vases and little tables along the hall.

Above this perch, a single 0.76 × 1.34 m fine timber lattice gives the arrival a human scale. Both walls and the ceiling become related warm mineral tones. Removing the high timber outline and the opposing decorative joints makes the joinery and door frames recede. About six metres later, one small woven work marks a second pause. The intervening wall stays bare. The fixed rear window is the final living picture; leave its sill clear.

The model leaves approximately 1.20 m between the seat edge and the opposite wall. This is an unoccupied geometric width, not a surveyed or accessibility-certified clearance. Someone putting on shoes temporarily uses part of it. Confirm the actual wall bay, door swings, storage opening and passage on site before fabrication. The entry closet remains in use; the small perch is for daily pairs.

## Fireplace

Keep the photographed paneled surround and the two framed sconces. Their proportions belong to the house. Bring the surround, inset stile panels and adjacent plaster closer together in tone; a warm chalk finish allows the dark firebox to remain the anchor. Retain the mantel profile rather than attaching a second, unrelated architectural surround.

Keep the space above the mantel empty. One low earthen vessel sits off centre on the existing shelf. No matching vases, extra lattice panels, decorative log stack or television above it. At night, the existing sconces should give two soft vertical pools, with their panels comfortably dim. The room does not need an evenly illuminated fireplace wall.

The proposed paint is a finish intention only. Select a coating appropriate to each actual substrate and the appliance manufacturer's temperature and clearance requirements. No new combustible trim has been modeled around the firebox.

## Where people sit, and what they see

The retained low sofa is turned diagonally toward the garden while keeping the fireplace in its left-hand view. The two Bellustar chairs remain a conversation pair; their swivel action lets a reader face the garden without rearranging the room. The table stays low and holds a shallow tea bowl, removing the generic branch arrangement from the middle of people's sightlines. A small reading table and shaded lamp sit by the sofa's garden end.

The main sofa garden views are rendered at a 1.10 m eye height, not from above the furniture. The garden-side chair also receives its own seated fireplace view. These are explicit viewing positions, not an ergonomic fit test for a particular occupant.

The four seasonal pictures use the same sofa position and lens. Maple foliage is changed or removed while trunks, branches and the evergreen framework remain. The sunlight directions are illustrative mood settings: they do not establish actual sun entry, a date or a time. The house dimensions and orientation are approximate. Species identity, seasonal leaf behaviour, drainage and planting health need confirmation against the actual garden.

Garden additions are three lighting intentions: one shielded tree accent, one modest downward pool near the bench, and a low step light. Existing trees, lawn, bench, paths and stone objects are retained. Avoid lighting every trunk or scattering illuminated bollards. Set a bedtime shutoff for decorative garden lights; keep useful entrance and step lighting separately controlled.

## Kumiko and concealed LEDs

Use a fine **senbon-inspired** grid here. The visual value is the timber edge and its shadow against a soft field. A complex pattern can work elsewhere, but this narrow hallway benefits from a simpler rhythm. [Tanihata's pattern guide](https://kumikowoodworking.com/design/) shows senbon among its traditional lattice options. This modeled panel is a custom dimensional study, not Tanihata fabrication CAD.

Specify warm white, high colour quality linear LEDs with smooth low-level dimming, in aluminium channels concealed inside a removable timber frame. Aim for 2700 K at useful output, warming toward about 2200 K for evening if the chosen system supports it. Conceal the LED dots and the bright channel itself from both standing and seated angles. Wash an internal pale reflector through an opal diffuser; do not expose strips on the front edge or trace the lattice with light.

The study models a shallow back box, diffuser, frame and concealed side channels. Its roughly 120 mm overall wall projection is a starting envelope, not a validated lighting section. Make a full-size sample of one bay before fixing depth: examine hotspots, shadow definition, spill around the frame and dimming behaviour. Allow an accessible driver, heat dissipation, service access and compatible electrical components. The modeled emission and light power communicate appearance; they are not fixture wattage or a lighting calculation.

As a technology reference, [Acuity's warm-dimming guidance](https://www.acuitybrands.com/resources/featured-technologies/mainstream-dynamic/warm-dimming) describes systems that shift from 2700 K toward 1800 K or 2000 K as they dim. This is evidence that the behaviour is available, not a product selection for this custom panel. Final colour range, driver and controls should be specified as one compatible assembly.

## Cleanup and maintenance

Use a removable, thin woven mat with a stable backing; avoid a loose fringe across the path. Make the seat cushion removable and the shoe compartment easy to wipe. Keep the lattice front removable for soft-brush cleaning and LED servicing. The garden should supply the changing ornament; occasional cut stems belong on the arrival tray or dining table only when they do not interfere with daily use. Do not fill every empty surface after the room is installed.
